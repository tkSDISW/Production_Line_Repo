## Where the name "PackML" comes from

**Purpose:** slide/reference material — the origin of the term itself, distinct from NOTE-0003's standards-body history.

### The name

PackML stands for **Packaging Machine Language**. Per OMAC's own chairman at the time (Bryan Griffen, in a Packaging World interview), the phrase was coined in the early days of the working group's effort to define a machine state model and information exchange between machines. It came about because packaging was the target application at the time. The name stuck and became a recognized brand even as the underlying technology later expanded well beyond packaging into robotics, converting, and printing — OMAC has since tried to shift emphasis toward "TR88" (ISA-TR88.00.02, the official standards document number), but "PackML" remains the name everyone actually uses in practice.

### Structural origin

In the early 2000s, OMAC's Packaging Work Group formed three technical sub-committees to unify how machines are introduced into the packaging market, each with a specific focus:
- **PackSoft** — research applicable programming languages for the packaging industry
- **PackConnect** — research applicable fieldbus networks for the packaging industry
- **PackML** — bridge the gap between PackSoft and PackConnect: define a common way to describe machine state and exchange data, independent of which fieldbus protocol carried it

So the "ML" is "Machine Language," not "Markup Language" — it follows the naming pattern of its sibling sub-committees (PackSoft, PackConnect), not an XML-style convention.
